How AirTrace works

AirTrace starts from the Copernicus CAMS global atmospheric-composition forecast — available everywhere, but often far off at city scale — and learns how to correct it from reference-grade ground monitors. It is validated only on cities it never saw during training, explains every forecast, and estimates where the pollution comes from.

Pipeline

From a global model to a city forecast

  1. 1

    Data sources

    CAMS global composition forecast (PM2.5, PM10, dust, aerosol optical depth, CO, NO₂, SO₂, O₃), ERA5 / forecast weather, AirNow reference monitors and NASA FIRMS fires.

  2. 2

    Daily features

    Hourly data become local-day features: CAMS means, night and peak values, day-over-day change, PM2.5/PM10 and SO₂/CO ratios, mixing height, ventilation, stagnation, humidity, rain, season and location.

  3. 3

    LightGBM correction

    Gradient-boosted trees predict log(1 + PM2.5) from those features. Two quantile models (10th and 90th percentile) give an 80% prediction range.

  4. 4

    SHAP drivers

    TreeSHAP splits every prediction into feature contributions, summed into human-readable groups such as “Air stagnation” or “Desert dust”.

  5. 5

    NMF source attribution

    Non-negative matrix factorisation of CAMS tracer patterns finds source signatures; a regression on observed PM2.5 turns them into source shares.

  6. 6

    Website

    Forecasts, drivers and sources are written as static JSON and served by GitHub Pages — no server, no tracking.

A GitHub Actions workflow re-runs steps 1–6 every 6 hours with the latest CAMS and weather forecasts.

Validation design

Tested the way it is used: on places without a monitor

Leave-location-out cross-validation

■ held-out locations   ■ training locations (one of the folds)

Monitors are grouped by location (1° latitude/longitude cells), and each group is held out in turn. Every score on this page comes from a model that had never seen that city, or any monitor near it — exactly the situation of a city without a monitor.

Temporal holdout

A second check trains on the earlier months and tests on the last months of the record, so the model is also judged on a season it has not seen.

Limitations & responsible AI

What AirTrace cannot do

Known limitations

  • City scale, not street scale. CAMS runs on a grid tens of kilometres wide. AirTrace forecasts a city-wide 24-hour average; a busy road or a local fire can be far worse.
  • Uneven training data. The monitors are mostly in North America and in capital cities with a US embassy. Regions with few training sites (see the per-region table) have larger and less certain errors.
  • Frozen in time. The model learned from one year. Where the embassy monitors went dark, we can no longer check whether it still holds — which is why every forecast carries an 80% range and the monitor status is always shown.
  • Sources are estimates. The attribution decomposes modelled tracer patterns, so it inherits CAMS emission-inventory errors; some validation checks fail and are shown as failing.
  • Forecast skill fades with lead time. Days 4–5 depend on weather forecasts that are themselves uncertain; the ranges widen accordingly.

Responsible use

  • Not medical advice. Health messages follow the US EPA AQI guidance. Always follow official advisories from local authorities.
  • Transparent by design. Every number on this site comes from the pipeline’s JSON output; validation failures are shown, not hidden. The code and model artifacts are public.
  • No personal data. The site is static: no accounts, cookies or tracking. It only uses open environmental data.
  • Equity. The people who lost their only reference monitor are the ones who most need a trustworthy estimate. AirTrace says clearly when a number is an estimate without a ground truth nearby.
  • Light footprint. Gradient-boosted trees train and run on an ordinary CPU — no GPU is needed for training or for the forecast runs.
